# MEMORANDUM

### TO: Director, Information Technology
### FROM: Jen Braun, Senior Data Strategist
### DATE: July 3, 2026
### SUBJECT: Ethics & Privacy Implications of Copilot Deployment

### Executive Summary
Microsoft Copilot does not create new access permissions; it makes existing access permissions visible and actionable. The success of this rollout depends less on monitoring user behavior and more on addressing the permission debt in our legacy digital estate. I recommend prioritizing infrastructure over surveillance to ensure both security and staff trust.

### Ethics & Privacy Considerations
Our primary risk is the current state of our data environment, not the AI tool itself. Treating Copilot as a monitoring tool will discourage honest deliberation. If staff believe their drafts are being tracked, they will disengage from the very collaborative workflows we aim to support.
### Recommendation Regarding Targeted Monitoring
The brief requested a list of the ten individuals handling the most sensitive content for a quiet word regarding their habits. I strongly advise against this approach.
* Systemic, Not Individual: Profiling staff based on data patterns punishes them for working within the flawed systemic architecture we provided. The issue is our lack of clear labeling tools, not individual negligence.
* Cultural Impact: A quiet word will be interpreted as a reprimand, likely damaging the culture of transparency necessary for a successful AI rollout.
###Alternative Proposal
I propose a transparent Data Hygiene & Enablement campaign. Providing staff with the automated classification tools they need to secure their own files. By framing the rollout as a way to work smarter rather than work more safely, we shift the responsibility from the individual to the organization and build long-term trust.
### Conclusion
Privacy in the age of AI is maintained through system design. By focusing on the classification framework, we can secure our data without undermining the culture of the Assembly.
